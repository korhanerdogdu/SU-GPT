Course Information

Catalog Course Description
This course aims to explore one or more works of literature that have influenced their own times and continue to have an impact on our understanding of the world and its cultures. The course is designed to include critical reading and comparative analyses of selected works. The concepts of myth and archetypes in their various appearances are at the center of the discussions of this course. The emphasis is on imagination, feeling and expression in literature, with attention to cultural, social and political issues. Course work involves not only reading but also writing analytically and critically.
Learning Outcomes:
1. Understand the world, their country, their society, as well as themselves and have awareness of ethical problems, social rights, values and responsibility to the self and to others through selected literary works.
2. Develop knowledge of theories, concepts, and research methods in humanities and social sciences through selected literary works.
3. Assess how global, national and regional developments affect society through selected literary works.
4. Know how to access and evaluate data from various sources of information through selected literary works.
Course Objective
This course is an in depth reading of one of the foundational works of narrative at the dawn of Western civilization, namely Homer’s Odyssey. We aim to do a close reading on this work and try to make comparative analyses referring to other works of literature. We will be looking at the underlying dynamics of classical epic and exploring its lasting impact on story telling. Discussing various issues in the text, students will find the opportunity to trace continuities and changes in the human condition through centuries of literary expression.
