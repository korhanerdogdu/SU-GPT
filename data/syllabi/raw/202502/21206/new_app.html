Course Information

Catalog Course Description
The graduation project is completed over two semesters through ENS 491 and ENS 492. It involves the design and implementation of an engineering solution to an open-ended problem, using the knowledge and skills acquired throughout the program. Projects are conducted in (preferably interdisciplinary) teams and require consideration of relevant constraints such as economic, environmental, societal, ethical, and sustainability factors, as well as the application of modern engineering practices. ENS 491 focuses on problem definition, analysis, and design, and includes workshops and case studies on engineering project development and design practices, carried out in interdisciplinary teams. ENS 492 emphasizes implementation, testing, and evaluation of the proposed solution. Students are expected to communicate their work effectively through written reports and oral presentations. Students must have completed at least 80 SU credits and satisfy the program-specific prerequisite requirements below: • Molecular Biology, Genetics, and Bioengineering: BIO 301 or BIO 303; • Computer Science and Engineering: CS 300 or CS 306 or CS 308 • Electronics Engineering: “EE 202 and ENS 211” (for students admitted to the university in 2026 and later), “EE 202 or ENS 211” (for students admitted to the university in 2025 and earlier); • Industrial Engineering: IE 305 or IE 312; • Materials Science and Nano Engineering: MAT 312 or MAT 314; • Mechatronics Engineering: Engineering: ME 301 or ME 303.
Learning Outcomes:
1. Ability to identify, define, formulate complex scientific/engineering problems/processes,
2. Experience in applying knowledge of science, mathematics and engineering to design a solution approach for a complex scientific/engineering problem under realistic constraints (such as economic, environmental, social, ethical, health and safety, manufacturability and sustainability), and in accordance with the standards of the field of specialty,
3. Ability to identify modern techniques and tools to solve a problem,
4. Knowledge on project management, risk management, change management, economic feasibility considerations, and legal consequences of engineering solutions,
5. Awareness on IP management, innovation and entrepreneurship,
6. experience in effective communication of their findings by reports and presentations, considering the diversity of target audience,
7. Experience in working in multidisciplinary teams,
8. Knowledge on Sustainable Development Goals (SDG), ability to map their project to relevant SDGs, and ability to evaluate sustainability impacts and economic feasibility using clearly stated assumptions/boundaries and measurable indicators.
Course Objective
Complex Problem: An open-ended problem including many components, which has possibly
alternative and nontrivial approaches/solutions by different students and which
* requires in-depth knowledge of the field of specialty (3 years of background gained so
far), abstract and creative thinking, use of research-based scientific/engineering
knowledge in a creative way,
* is relevant to stakeholders with varying needs and constraints.
Complex System, Process, Device or Product: A system, process, device or a product, which
has multiple components and subsystems, possibly of relevance to more than one discipline,
whose analysis and design is a complex problem.
Major Design Component: Engineering design is a systematic intelligent process in which
students should generate, evaluate and specify solutions for devices, systems and processes
whose form(s) and function(s) achieve certain objectives and users' needs while satisfying a
specified set of constraints. A capstone design project adheres to realistic constraints and is
based on following fundamental elements: Objectives, criteria, synthesis, analysis,
construction, testing and evaluation.
