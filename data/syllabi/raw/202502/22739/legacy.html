Course Information

Catalog Course Description
Today’s machines are intelligent, and they are transforming consumption and consumer experiences. For example, AI-powered algorithms now influence a remarkable range of consumption choices such as what we listen to, wear, watch, read, drink, and so on (consider Spotify, Stitch Fix, Netflix). With AI-driven adoption in consumer product industries is expected to reach new heights, this course aims to provide new perspectives on the wider relationship between humans and machines. We will acknowledge that AI can help marketers leverage customer data and improve marketing performance, but we will also study the risks for consumer and societal well- being. The course combines a variety of theoretical perspectives including management, psychology, art, sociology, and engineering.
Learning Outcomes:
Course Objective
Upon completion of this course, students should be able to
-sharpen their understanding of how artificial intelligence and algorithms are utilized in various facets of marketing, from personalized recommendations to predictive analytics.
-critically assess everyday consumer challenges and recognize how algorithms can provide innovative solutions to enhance consumer experiences.
-evaluate the potential impacts of AI, both positive and negative, on individual and societal wellbeing, considering factors such as privacy, ethics, and economic implications.
-acquire practical skills in applying AI systems responsibly to benefit individuals, society, and the environment, emphasizing ethical and sustainable practices.
-improve decision-making abilities by understanding the trade-offs and ethical considerations inherent in AI-driven marketing strategies.
-enhance communication skills, both written and verbal, to effectively convey insights and ideas related to AI and its impact on marketing,
