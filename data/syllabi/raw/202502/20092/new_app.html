Course Information

Catalog Course Description
This course is designed to broaden and deepen the students' understanding of cultural theories and to develop their ability to think critically about cultural issues through a sustained engagement with a selection of works by some of the major thinkers of the twentieth century. The questions raised in the course will concern the ways in which these theories have shaped contemporary research and pedagogical agendas, the negotiations and interventions they have enabled, their social and political contexts, and to what extent they can "travel" across cultures.
Learning Outcomes:
1. Upon completion of this course students should be able to: 1. Identify key strengths in the contribution of each cultural theorist 2. Identify the limitations of the perspectives of each cultural theorist 3. Compare the strengths and limitations of concepts that are introduced 4. Systematically apply at least two theoretical concepts to a research object
Course Objective
This course is designed to explore major works and concepts in Cultural Studies and their theoretical contributions in social sciences. How were those major works and issues interpreted and reinterpreted in different ways by different scholars? How did the context and zeitgeist affect the formation of those works? How can we think of Cultural Studies as an accumulation of a large body of knowledge, along with continuities and breaks, in terms of adhering to certain principles and adopting the new trends in social sciences? Together, we will discuss possible answers to these questions, and with a focus on interactions between Cultural Studies, and other Social Science disciplines, such as Anthropology and Sociology.
