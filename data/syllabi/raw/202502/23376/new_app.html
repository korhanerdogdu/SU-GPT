Course Information

Catalog Course Description
This course introduces the fundamental concepts, methods, and applications of business analytics with a focus on data-driven decision making in various sectors. The course also provides a brief overview of big data analytics as an emerging area and its implications for business. Analytical techniques such as data visualization, statistical analysis, data mining, optimization, and simulation will be covered. supported by practical case studies. Practical sessions and case studies aim to complement lectures to advance both conceptual understanding of the business analytics methods and develop the pertinent skills.
Learning Outcomes:
1. Define Business Analytics and its role and contributions in decision making
Course Objective
Upon successful completion of the course, the student should be able to:
1. Define business analytics and its role and contributions in decision-making,
2. Describe methodological approaches to business analytics and their application contexts,
3. Use descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analytics methods on business data under corresponding decision-making contexts,
4. Analyze a business case, select and apply an appropriate method to reach a business decision,
5. Understand the nature of big data and how it can be exploited to create value,
6. Use at least one leading software package in data analytics.

Course Materials

Resources:
Textbook:

Jeffrey D. Camm; James J. Cochran; Michael J. Fry; Jeffrey W. Ohlmann, Business Analytics, 5th Edition, Cengage Learning, © 2024.


Case Studies:

Cases are used as real-world examples of the topics. There will be three case analysis reports to be prepared as a team. The teams can consist of up to three students (teams comprised of one student will also be allowed). The teams should work to address the issues raised in the case, clearly identify the problems to be studied, determine and apply the necessary tools and prepare a formal case report.


Reading Assignments:

Some additional articles and cases might be announced and used as real-world examples of the topics covered in class.


HBSP Course Pack:

Some of the reading assignments as well as case studies will be accessible through BAN 500 course pack on Harvard Business School Publishing (HBSP). BAN 500 students who are not already a HBSP member will be requested to register via their SU email addresses. Hopefully, similar to previous years, the contents of our course pack will be provided by SBS.

The course syllabus, announcements, the details of the case assignments, reflection paper and exams as well as your grades will be posted on SUCourse. Students are expected to visit SUCourse regularly each week. 

The announcements component of SUCourse will be actively used to share announcements. Each announcement posted on SUCourse will be emailed to the students, thus students are responsible for regularly checking their emails and appropriately setting emailing options in their SUCourse account. 

Some of the in-class exercises might be downloaded and uploaded via the Assignments component. Similarly, the case analysis reports are to be uploaded under the designated SUCourse assignments.

During the grading of the case reports and reflection paper, the Turnitin tool will be used. Sabanci University uses a very powerful web-based tool called Turnitin. Turnitin is the worldwide standard in online plagiarism prevention and also provides checks for the use of generative AI. It allows instructors to compare student papers against a database composed of millions of articles. Turnitin will scan each case analysis report, and if the results are unacceptable, they will be reflected in your grades by applying penalties for plagiarism and misuse of generative AI.
